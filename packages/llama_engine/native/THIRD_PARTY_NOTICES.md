# Third-party notices: libmozhi_llama

`libmozhi_llama.so`, `libmozhi_llama_dotprod.so` and
`libmozhi_llama_i8mm.so` (shipped in the APK; the last two for arm64-v8a
only) are built from the sources below, vendored as a pinned git submodule
under `third_party/` and compiled statically: llama.cpp's inference of
large language models in GGUF files, on the CPU backend of ggml. The three
libraries differ only in the CPU instructions ggml's kernels use.
Everything here is under permissive licenses; nothing is GPL or
proprietary, and there is no Google code. The app (MIT) must show these
notices (on its licenses page).

Not compiled in: llama.cpp's `common/` library (and with it its Jinja
engine, downloads and HTTP code), tools, examples, server and web UI,
multimodal support (`mtmd`), and the headers it vendors for those
(nlohmann/json, cpp-httplib, miniaudio, stb_image, subprocess.h, the
SHA-1/SHA-256/xxHash code); ggml's GPU and accelerator backends (CUDA,
Vulkan, OpenCL, Metal, SYCL, Hexagon, ...), BLAS, OpenMP, llamafile's
sgemm, KleidiAI and LLGuidance; libcurl and OpenSSL. No source file is
modified.

The language **models** are never bundled: they are downloaded at run time
(only when the user asks) from the sources of the app's model catalogue
(`../assets/models.json`, or a mirror) or imported from files, and keep
their own licenses (Hy-MT2 1.8B by Tencent, Gemma 4 E2B by Google and
Qwen3 1.7B by the Qwen team: Apache-2.0; listed in the catalogue and on the
app's licenses page). The tests' tiny models (`../tool/make_test_model.py`)
borrow the vocabulary of `third_party/llama.cpp/models/ggml-vocab-llama-spm.gguf`
and are never shipped.

The `mozhi_llama` shim itself (`src/`, `tool/`) is MIT, like the app.
